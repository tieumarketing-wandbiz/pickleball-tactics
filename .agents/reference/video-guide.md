## What the video is (and isn't)
A beginner **rules** guide for doubles pickleball (serve rules, two-bounce rule, kitchen rules, scoring), English, one
right-handed narrator, plus real doubles gameplay 6:27–9:27. It does **not** coach technique. Mechanics below are measured
from demo clips, so treat numbers as estimates. Clearly visible: serve, dink, volley/punch/block, smash, ready stance,
lunges. **Not shown**: drive, drop, lob, reset (only 8:51.8), speedup, roll, flick, ATP, Erne, backhands — keep the current
animation for those.

## Rules that constrain animation
- Serve: **underhand**, contact below the waist, paddle head below the wrist, both feet behind the baseline, one foot always
  grounded (no jump). Drop serve = ball released from ~shoulder height, never thrown down.
- Two-bounce rule: serving team stays back; receiving team one back, one up.
- No volley with a foot on/in the kitchen; momentum carrying a volleyer/overhead into the kitchen afterwards is a fault.
  After a bounced ball in the kitchen a player may step in, then must get both feet back out.

Ready paddle: sternum height (~1.1–1.3 m), 0.25–0.35 m in front of chest, face square, head tilted up 45–70°, elbows ~90°.
Wide reach: lunge with stance ~2.5× shoulder width, trailing leg near-straight, pelvis −25–30 cm, paddle within 0.2 m of ground; recover with shuffles, not crossovers.
Paddle head **below** wrist for serve and dink, **above** wrist for ready, volley, smash. One-handed grip near the butt of the handle (continental-ish).

## Implications for the rig (stage A/B)
The rig must deform cleanly at these extremes: knee flexion up to ~90° (dink squat) and deep lunges (pelvis −30 cm),
shoulder flexion ~150° with the off arm up (smash), a fully extended overhead hitting arm, and hip/shoulder turn of ~45–60°.
Check shoulders, armpits, hips/groin and knees specifically at those poses.
